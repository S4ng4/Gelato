ORIGINS

When Ice
Became Art

Gelato was born in the Florentine Renaissance, around 1565, when architect Bernardo Buontalenti was commissioned by the Medici to create an extraordinary dessert for court celebrations. Combining cream, honey, and snow from the Apennines, the first modern version of gelato was born.

In the following centuries, Sicilian gelato makers brought the tradition across Europe — to Paris, Vienna, Madrid. It was in Naples in the 18th century that gelato left the royal courts to become food of the people, served in cones on the streets.

Today, Maestro Stefano Biasini — Italian Gelato Champion and international World Press Champion — brings this centuries-old tradition to the heart of Philadelphia, reinterpreting every flavor with the precision of an engineer and the sensitivity of an artist.

THE TECHNIQUE

The Art of Churning

La Churning is the soul of gelato: the process that transforms simple ingredients into a one-of-a-kind sensory experience. Every detail is controlled, every variable is calculated.

🥛
01
Selection

Only fresh local whole milk, selected sugars and Italian DOP ingredients. No additives, no preservatives. Quality starts with the raw ingredients.

🔥
02
Pasteurization

The base mix is pasteurized at a controlled temperature, then aged for 6–12 hours. This process develops the creamy structure characteristic of artisanal gelato.

❄️
03
Churning

In the churning machine, the mix cools rapidly while incorporating only 20–30% airOverrun: the percentage of air incorporated.
Less air = more density = more flavor. — half of what industrial ice cream contains. Result: unique density and intensity.

🍨
04
Service

Stored at −11°C (warmer than ice cream), the gelato is served at the optimal temperature: soft enough to release all its aromas, firm enough to hold the cone.
